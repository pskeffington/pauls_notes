# How the biological domains connect

Focused supporting reference for the [Breast Cancer Research Guide — People, Data, Biology and 60 Genes](BREAST_CANCER_60_GENE_COMPANION.md).

[Companion](BREAST_CANCER_60_GENE_COMPANION.md) · [Biological processes](GENE_BIOLOGY_LEARNING_GUIDE.md) · [Gene index](BREAST_CANCER_60_GENE_COMPANION.md#chapter-6-the-integrated-60-gene-atlas)

A cell coordinates information, growth, repair and survival while interacting with its surroundings. The 12 domains are a teaching map of that coordination. They are not mutually exclusive modules or a reconstructed molecular interaction network. Gene-specific normal-function records are linked on the cards; the [category network figure](FIGURES/README.md#category-network) illustrates membership rather than binding.

## An evidence exercise

Choose two linked genes. Write a hypothesis connecting them, then state whether the hypothesis concerns direct binding, regulation, shared cell identity or response to a common environment. These are different hypotheses requiring different experiments. An association network can nominate an experiment but cannot silently become a mechanistic network.

Now read one card's primary source and identify exactly what was manipulated or observed. If only the abstract is reviewed, record that limitation before proposing a follow-up. The [interpreting-evidence diagram](FIGURES/README.md#interpreting-evidence) compares evidence types without portraying an automatic progression to clinical use.
